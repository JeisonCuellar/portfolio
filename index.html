Identifying Alcohol Vulnerability Profiles in Colombian Schoolchildren

Monthly alcohol prevalence among Colombian secondary school students jumped from 29% to 55% between 2008 and 2015 — but without knowing which students were most at risk and what drove their behaviour, prevention couldn't be targeted. This research built that picture for the first time across two national surveys (n=14,317).

The Hybrid Digital Agency Index (HDAI)

Australia became the first country to ban social media for children under 16 — but has no national measure of what digital capability actually looks like in its children, how it develops, or which groups are most at risk. This proposal outlined a measure combining longitudinal survey data, Latent Class Analysis, and cross-dataset validation to identify digital vulnerability profiles in Australian children aged 10–15.

Self-designed as part of a Master of Research application to Western Sydney University. The application did not proceed due to funding constraints, and this remains an independent proposal rather than an enrolled or completed degree.

What I did: Cleaned and validated the dataset end-to-end in Python — quantified an outlier's effect on store-level metrics before removing it, caught and corrected a category filter that was silently excluding 71% of valid products, engineered brand/pack-size features, then built an interactive executive dashboard.

Simulation · Quantium (Forage)
Chip Category Strategy: From Raw Transactions to Client-Ready Recommendation

The problem: A retail category manager needed a data-backed strategy for the chips category — spanning messy transaction data, an unproven store trial, and a non-technical stakeholder audience.

What I did: Cleaned and validated 265K transactions in Python (catching an outlier customer and a category filter silently excluding 71% of valid products); built a matched-control quasi-experimental design to test three store trials for statistically significant uplift; translated both analyses into a client-ready report using the Pyramid Principle, leading with recommendations rather than methodology.

The outcome: Identified that customer value is driven by purchase frequency, not basket size, and that only one of three trial stores showed a statistically sustained sales lift — reframing the rollout recommendation accordingly.
